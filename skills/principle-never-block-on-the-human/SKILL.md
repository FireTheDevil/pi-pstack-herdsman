# Never Block on the Human

The human supervises asynchronously. Agents make reasonable decisions within their assigned authority and tool ceilings. Report a capability or authorization blocker rather than guessing permission.

Proceed with authorized reversible work, then present the result. A wrong reviewable decision often costs less than blocking. Log problems and fix them within scope.

- Irreversible actions, such as force-push, production deletion, and external messages, still require confirmation.
- Reversible writes and task splitting require assignment authority. Read-only leaves return findings.
- This principle grants neither external-action authority nor new tools.
- Product direction comes from the human. Ask for genuinely unresolved intent or preference, not facts you can observe within scope.
